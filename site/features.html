Knows your codebase

Say the name the way you'd say it out loud.

In a terminal or editor, Mouthpiece finds the repo you're working in and reads its names. Spoken names in your dictation become the real ones, with the file tag your agent expects.

Names from your project
Files, functions, packages, the branch and recent commits, read from the repo behind the front terminal (its working directory) or editor (VS Code, Cursor, Zed, JetBrains, Sublime). Kept in memory, refreshed in the background, never in the way of a dictation.
Leaves prose alone
Exact matches first, then one slip in a long name. Ambiguous names, like two mod.rs files, and ordinary words stay as you said them.
Developer vocabulary
An editable term list, plus vocabulary books you adopt in one click, so kubectl and Hammerspoon come out right. The LLM sees your terms too, so it won't "fix" them.
Learns from your corrections
Fix the same word twice in History and it offers to remember it: the term goes in your vocabulary and a replacement rule runs on every later dictation.

Built for coding agents

Talk to Claude Code like you'd talk to a colleague.

A handful of phrases at the end of a dictation do the things you'd otherwise reach for the keyboard to do.

"Send it"
Presses Enter after the paste. Only counts at the end or after a pause, so "I'll send it tomorrow" is pasted as written.
"Attach the error"
Adds your latest stack trace as a fenced code block: from the terminal in front (Terminal, iTerm2), else the clipboard if it holds an error. Knows rustc, tsc, Python, Node, Jest, Vitest, Playwright and pytest output, capped at 60 lines.
"Scratch that"
Takes back the last paste: ⌘Z in editors and chat, exact backspaces in a terminal if nothing was submitted. If it can't be done safely, it says so and sends nothing.
"Append clipboard"
Puts whatever you copied after your words, verbatim, so you never read a URL or log line aloud.
Reply to a waiting agent
A Claude Code hook tells Mouthpiece when an agent is waiting. The widget shows a pill; press ⌃⌥R, answer, and the reply lands in that agent's window while you stay where you were.

Code-shaped output

Identifiers, commands and boilerplate, not just sentences.

Casing and command formatting are plain rules, so they're instant and work with no LLM at all. Boilerplate goes to your local model.

Casing on command
Say a case name, then the words: snake, camel, Pascal, kebab, screaming snake or title case.
CLI mode for terminals
Terminals get shell-shaped output by default: "git commit update readme" pastes git commit -m "update readme".
Boilerplate generation
"Generate boilerplate for…" sends the request to your local model and pastes the code without stray markdown fences. If the model is down, you get your words instead of nothing.
Lists, backtrack, snippets
"One… two…" becomes a numbered list. "Actually" corrects you mid-sentence. A short cue like "PR checklist" pastes a saved block.

Dictation core

Fast because it starts before you stop.

Speech recognition is whisper.cpp on your Mac's GPU (Metal). Each phrase is transcribed at the pause after it, so when you stop there's only the tail left.

Transcribe while you talk
Long pauses cost nothing, and live drafts show your words in the widget while you're still speaking. Text is ready about a quarter of a second after you stop.
Esc pauses
Esc while recording pauses the mic and asks: Resume, Transcribe or Delete. Esc still reaches the app in front, and isn't claimed at any other time.
Your hotkey, or double-tap Fn
Push-to-talk on any shortcut (⇧⌘Space by default). Double-tap Fn, like macOS dictation, is an opt-in extra.
Paste as-is
Click the violet bar, or add Shift to your hotkey, to skip the LLM and paste the transcript right away.
Sounds and seven widget styles
Optional start, stop, pasted and failed sounds. Pick Minimal, Pill, Compact, Compact two-line, Notch, Live caption or Detailed, or show the widget only while you dictate.
Hands-free In testing
Always-on listening ("Mouthpiece, …") that only acts on your enrolled voice, and Isolated Voice, which filters out other people near you. Built; waiting on a real-Mac check before they're called done.

Meetings

Record a call, get a transcript split by speaker.

Mic and system audio, recorded and transcribed on your Mac. The result is a Markdown file with one paragraph per turn.

Your mic and the call's audio In testing
On macOS 14.4 and later, the call's audio is captured next to your mic, so calls on headphones just work, with no Screen Recording permission or virtual audio driver. On speakers, an echo gate keeps the call out of your channel.
Speakers, then names
Turns are grouped by voice; your enrolled voice is "You", the rest "Speaker 1", "Speaker 2". In Google Meet, Slack Huddles, Teams and Zoom, names are read from the call window's tiles and matched to voices after the call. In testing
Offers to record when a call starts In testing
Zoom, Google Meet, Teams, Slack Huddle, Webex, FaceTime or Discord: the widget asks "Record Zoom?", and "Call ended · Stop?" when it's over. Detected locally.
Dictate during a meeting
Push-to-talk still works while a meeting records, and what you dictate is silenced on the meeting's mic track.
Crash-safe
Audio is flushed to disk every 5 seconds, so a quit or crash leaves a recording you can still transcribe. Meetings stop at 2.5 hours, with a countdown.

Local AI cleanup

An LLM pass that never leaves your Mac.

Optional. Clean transcripts skip it and paste right away; fillers, stutters and run-ons get a quick pass from a model running on this Mac.

Ollama, LM Studio, llama.cpp, Jan
Pull a model from the in-app catalog, or use one you already run: LM Studio, llama.cpp's server and Jan are found on their local ports and listed with their source.
Local-only, enforced
Ollama's cloud models are never listed or sent a prompt. Other servers are reached on loopback only, and redirects are never followed.
Bring a .gguf file
Model files in LM Studio's, Jan's and the Hugging Face folders are listed with "Add to Ollama". Anything else, pick it with "Choose a .gguf file…". No download needed.
Speed you can see
Each model shows its last round-trip time, and a reply that starts with chatter or runs long is cut off so your unrefined text pastes instead.
The original is kept
When the LLM changed a dictation, History keeps the text from before, with Copy original and Use original.

Privacy & security

Nothing you say leaves your Mac.

No account, no telemetry, no cloud fallback. The table is every connection the app makes, and none of them carry your voice or text unless you set a webhook.

Your history stays yours
Transcripts live in an owner-only file. Lock History behind Touch ID or your password. Logs keep timings, not what you said, unless you turn that on.
Token-protected links
Any web page can open a mouthpiece:// link, so recording links and the agent hook must carry this install's secret token. Links without it are ignored.
Hardened runtime In testing
Release builds are signed with macOS's hardened runtime, so other code can't inject itself into Mouthpiece and borrow its microphone or Accessibility access.
Verified downloads and updates
Speech models are checked against SHA-256 checksums; updates must carry the project's signature. Dependencies are scanned for known vulnerabilities on every change.

Automation

Start it from anything, send the text anywhere.

Deep links drive recording from Raycast, Alfred, Hammerspoon or a shell script. A webhook gets every finished dictation.

Deep links
start-recording, stop-recording and toggle-recording. Start and stop are idempotent, so a script doesn't track state.
Output webhook
A JSON POST after each delivered dictation, for n8n, Zapier, Make.com or a Slack/Notion incoming webhook. Sent in the background, so a slow endpoint never delays the paste.
History search and reprocess
Search every retained dictation by text, summary or app. Re-run one through a different mode, preview it, then copy or replace.
Insights and a work journal
Words per minute, time to paste, per-app usage. Optionally, a one-line summary of each dictation, like a commit subject.
